# Maturity Stages: Deep Reference

Detailed definition of each maturity stage. Use this reference when you need the full picture of what each stage looks like, what capabilities are available, and what mistakes are common.

---

## Stage 1: Nascent (<15 conversions/month)

### What This Stage Looks Like
- Account may be newly created or inherited with minimal history
- Conversion tracking may be incomplete or unverified
- Campaign structure is basic: perhaps one Search campaign and one PMax or Display campaign
- Keyword lists are small, match types may be default (broad)
- Budget is often modest ($500-$3,000/month)
- Limited or no remarketing lists
- No structured testing or optimization cadence

### Available Capabilities
- Manual CPC bidding (appropriate and recommended for control)
- Maximize Clicks (for traffic and data collection phase)
- Maximize Conversions without target (if 5+ conversions to learn from)
- Basic negative keywords (universal negatives list)
- Standard Search campaigns
- Simple PMax with limited asset groups
- Location and language targeting
- Basic ad extensions (sitelinks, callouts)

### Restricted Capabilities (Don't Recommend)
- Target CPA/ROAS: algorithm needs 15+ conversions in a 30-day window to optimize. Below that, targets cause erratic bidding and suppressed delivery.
- Portfolio bidding: individual campaigns aren't stable enough.
- Complex PMax segmentation: not enough data to evaluate channel distribution.
- Value-based bidding: requires reliable conversion value data AND volume.
- Audience-only campaigns: lists are too small (need 1,000+ for Search, 100+ for Display/YouTube).
- Experiments/A-B tests: insufficient traffic for statistical significance in reasonable timeframes.

### Common Mistakes at This Stage
1. **Premature automation:** Agency sets tCPA immediately. Algorithm has no data, delivery craters.
2. **Over-segmentation:** Creating 10 campaigns for 10 conversions/month. Each campaign starves.
3. **Ignoring tracking:** Optimizing campaigns before verifying conversions are real.
4. **PMax as default:** Running PMax without sufficient assets or feed, resulting in budget going to low-quality Display placements.
5. **Broad match without negatives:** "Let Google figure it out" without guardrails burns budget on irrelevant terms.

### Key Metrics to Monitor
- Conversion tracking accuracy (are conversions real?)
- Search term relevance (are clicks qualified?)
- Click volume trend (is the account growing?)
- CPC levels (are bids competitive enough for visibility?)
- Landing page conversion rate (is the page working?)

### Graduation Criteria (Move to Developing)
- Consistently hitting 15+ primary conversions/month for 2+ consecutive months
- Conversion tracking verified and stable
- Basic campaign structure in place (brand separated from non-brand)
- At least 3 months of active management history

## Stage 2: Developing (15-50 conversions/month)

### What This Stage Looks Like
- Account has proven it can generate conversions consistently
- Automated bidding is being tested or recently adopted
- Campaign structure is expanding (brand vs. non-brand, some product segmentation)
- Search term mining is producing regular negative additions
- Remarketing lists are building but may still be small
- Budget is moderate ($3,000-$10,000/month typically)
- Some PMax testing underway

### Available Capabilities
- Maximize Conversions (stable at this volume)
- tCPA with conservative targets (set 15-20% above actual CPA to avoid suppression)
- tROAS with conservative targets (set 15-20% below actual ROAS)
- PMax with standard asset groups
- Standard Shopping campaigns
- Demand Gen testing
- Audience signals in PMax
- Expanded keyword strategy (testing phrase and broad match with negatives)
- Feed optimization (if Merchant Center connected)
- Basic remarketing (site visitors, converters)

### Restricted Capabilities (Don't Recommend Yet)
- Aggressive targets: setting tCPA at or below current CPA chokes volume.
- VBB: conversion volume insufficient for value signal learning.
- Portfolio bidding: individual campaigns may not be stable enough to pool.
- Complex audience segmentation: remarketing lists may be below minimum sizes.
- Multiple simultaneous experiments: not enough traffic to run concurrent tests.
- PMax channel-level optimization: not enough data in each channel for meaningful analysis.

### Common Mistakes at This Stage
1. **Target too aggressive:** Agency sets tCPA at current CPA. Algorithm can't hit it consistently, enters perpetual learning.
2. **Too many campaign types:** Launching PMax + Shopping + Display + YouTube simultaneously dilutes the limited conversion data.
3. **Ignoring search terms:** Account has enough volume for patterns to emerge but nobody's mining them.
4. **Not testing automation:** Sticking with Manual CPC when there's enough data to test Maximize Conversions.
5. **Neglecting feed:** Merchant Center connected but product titles are raw manufacturer text.

### Key Metrics to Monitor
- Conversion volume trend (approaching 50/month?)
- CPA/ROAS stability (is automated bidding settling?)
- Search term waste rate (% of spend on irrelevant terms)
- Impression share (is the account competitive?)
- PMax channel distribution (if running PMax)
- Audience list sizes (reaching minimums?)

### Graduation Criteria (Move to Established)
- Consistently hitting 50+ primary conversions/month for 2+ consecutive months
- Automated bidding (tCPA or tROAS) running stably for 30+ days
- Enhanced conversions enabled and verified
- Campaign structure supports the business model (not over- or under-segmented)
- At least 6 months of active management history

## Stage 3: Established (50-100 conversions/month)

### What This Stage Looks Like
- Automated bidding is the norm, with defined targets
- Campaign structure is mature and intentional
- Regular optimization cadence (weekly search terms, monthly creative review)
- Remarketing lists at healthy sizes
- Feed is maintained (if applicable)
- Budget is substantial enough for meaningful testing ($10,000-$50,000/month typically)
- Multiple campaign types running in coordination

### Available Capabilities
- Full suite of Smart Bidding strategies
- Target-based bidding with performance-based targets
- Portfolio bidding (group related campaigns)
- Campaign experiments (A/B testing)
- Full PMax optimization (8-channel analysis, asset group testing)
- Product tier management (Heroes, Sidekicks, Zombies, Villains)
- Creative A/B testing with statistical significance
- Audience expansion (lookalike, custom intent)
- Budget reallocation modeling
- Competitive analysis (auction insights, IS strategy)
- Feed optimization (title testing, custom labels)
- YouTube and Demand Gen with dedicated strategies

### Still Restricted
- VBB: possible if approaching 100 conversions/month with reliable value data, but test carefully.
- Incrementality testing: typically needs 100+ conversions and geographic diversity.
- Marginal efficiency modeling: more useful at advanced volumes.

### Common Mistakes at This Stage
1. **Complacency:** "It's working" leads to coasting. Established accounts can always improve.
2. **Over-optimization:** Changing targets weekly. Give the algorithm time (14-day minimum between changes).
3. **Ignoring cannibalization:** Multiple campaign types competing for the same users without coordination.
4. **Not testing creative:** Enough traffic for meaningful tests, but running the same RSAs for months.
5. **Budget ceiling:** Not modeling what additional budget could yield. Leaving constrained campaigns capped.

### Key Metrics to Monitor
- CPA/ROAS trend (improving, stable, or degrading?)
- Impression share and Lost IS breakdown (Budget vs. Rank)
- Creative performance (CTR trends, ad strength, asset ratings)
- PMax channel distribution shifts
- Product performance tiers (if Shopping)
- Audience performance by segment
- Competitive positioning (auction insights trends)

### Graduation Criteria (Move to Advanced)
- Consistently hitting 100+ primary conversions/month
- Full-stack tracking: enhanced conversions + offline import (if applicable)
- Stable portfolio or target-based bidding across all campaigns
- At least 12 months of active management history
- Campaign naming conventions and labels in active use

---

## Stage 4: Advanced (100+ conversions/month)

### What This Stage Looks Like
- High conversion volume provides rich signal for all optimization
- Sophisticated bidding (VBB, portfolio strategies, experiments)
- Granular campaign structure with clear naming conventions
- Full-stack conversion tracking (enhanced + offline)
- Regular testing cadence (creative, audiences, bidding, landing pages)
- Budget is significant ($50,000+/month in many cases)
- All relevant campaign types active and coordinated
- Data connections complete (GA4, Merchant Center, YouTube, CRM)

### Available Capabilities
Everything from prior stages, plus:
- Value-based bidding (optimize for profit/margin, not just conversions)
- Marginal efficiency modeling (diminishing returns analysis by campaign)
- Incrementality testing (geo-based experiments, holdout groups)
- Cross-channel attribution analysis
- Portfolio bidding optimization
- Advanced PMax (channel-level efficiency, placement pruning, asset experiments)
- Multivariate creative testing
- Custom scripts and automated rules
- Market expansion modeling (new geos, audiences, channels)
- Competitor conquest strategies
- Offline conversion optimization (pipeline value weighting)

### Common Mistakes Even at This Stage
1. **Assuming tracking is perfect:** Advanced accounts still need regular tracking audits. Drift happens.
2. **Negative keyword debt:** Years of accumulated negatives with no conflict audit. New keywords get blocked by old negatives.
3. **PMax opacity:** Trusting PMax channel allocation without periodic auditing.
4. **Learning period cascade:** Changing multiple campaigns simultaneously, putting 30% of the account in learning.
5. **Attribution overconfidence:** DDA is better than last-click, but it's still a model with limitations.

### Key Metrics to Monitor
- Marginal CPA/ROAS (efficiency of the last dollar spent)
- Portfolio performance vs. individual campaign targets
- VBB value accuracy (predicted vs. actual conversion values)
- Incrementality signals (branded search lift from upper-funnel)
- Cross-campaign cannibalization
- Learning period exposure (% of budget in learning campaigns)
- Competitive dynamics (IS trends, CPC inflation)

### No Graduation Beyond This
Advanced is the final stage, but sophistication within Advanced varies enormously. An Advanced account managing $50K/month looks different from one managing $500K/month. The principles are the same; the marginal gains and testing velocity differ.

# Video Creative Framework

Evaluation framework for video creative in YouTube and Demand Gen campaigns.

---

## Google's ABCD Framework

Google's research-backed framework for effective video advertising:

### A: Attention
- Hook within the first 5 seconds. For skippable ads, these 5 seconds determine whether users stay or skip.
- Brand or problem statement visible immediately
- Movement and action in the opening frame (static images lose attention)
- Audio hook that complements the visual (music, voice, sound effect)
- Avoid slow builds. The "cinematic reveal" approach loses most viewers before the reveal.

### B: Branding
- Brand name or logo visible within the first 5 seconds AND at the end
- Audio brand mention (name spoken aloud) increases recall
- Consistent brand colors and visual identity throughout
- For short-form (bumper/6s): brand present throughout the entire ad, not just at the end
- For long-form: brand integration throughout, not just bookend mentions

### C: Connection
- Emotional hook or relatable scenario that resonates with the target audience
- Human faces increase connection and attention (eye-tracking data supports this)
- Story arc appropriate to format length: 6s = single moment, 15s = setup + payoff, 30s+ = full narrative
- Audio and visual working together (not contradicting or redundant)
- Humor, surprise, or empathy as connection drivers

### D: Direction
- Clear CTA at the end of the video
- Tell the viewer exactly what to do next ("Visit our site," "Shop now," "Sign up free")
- CTA should match the campaign objective (awareness CTA differs from action CTA)
- End card with URL, button, or next step visible for at least 3 seconds
- Don't bury the CTA mid-video where most viewers have already dropped off

## Format Requirements

| Format | Duration | Aspect Ratio | Skippable | Primary Use Case |
|--------|----------|-------------|-----------|-----------------|
| **Bumper** | 6 seconds | 16:9 | No | Awareness, frequency, brand recall |
| **In-stream skippable** | 15s-3min (skip after 5s) | 16:9 | Yes (after 5s) | Consideration, action, remarketing |
| **In-stream non-skip** | 15 seconds | 16:9 | No | Awareness, reach, key messaging |
| **In-feed** | Any (thumbnail click) | 16:9 or 1:1 | N/A (user-initiated) | Consideration, education |
| **YouTube Shorts** | Up to 60s (vertical) | 9:16 | N/A (feed-based) | Awareness, reach, younger audiences |

### Format Selection by Campaign Objective
- **Awareness:** Bumper (frequency) + non-skip (reach) combination
- **Consideration:** In-stream skippable (15-30s) for story and education
- **Action/Conversion:** In-stream skippable (15-60s) with strong CTA
- **Remarketing:** In-stream skippable, shorter (15-30s), focused on offer/CTA

---

## Duration Strategy by Objective

### Awareness (6-15 seconds)
- Keep it short and high-frequency
- Single message, single visual impression
- Brand recall over detailed messaging
- Works best in bumper and non-skip formats

### Consideration (15-30 seconds)
- Enough time for a story arc: problem, solution, proof
- Feature or benefit demonstration
- Testimonial or use-case walkthrough
- In-stream skippable is the primary format

### Action (15-60 seconds)
- Problem statement, solution demonstration, social proof, CTA
- Longer format allows for objection handling
- Multiple CTAs throughout (mid-roll and end)
- Remarketing audiences can handle longer formats (they already know you)

## Hook Methodology (First 5 Seconds)

The opening hook determines whether users watch or skip. Five proven hook types:

### 1. Question Hook
"What if you could [desirable outcome]?"
Engages curiosity. Works when the question addresses a real pain point. Avoid generic questions.

### 2. Problem Hook
Show the pain point immediately: frustration, failure, inconvenience. No setup needed. Jump straight into the problem the viewer recognizes.

### 3. Surprise Hook
Unexpected visual or statement that breaks expectations. A startling statistic, a counter-intuitive claim, or a visual that doesn't match what the viewer anticipated.

### 4. Social Proof Hook
"10,000 businesses switched to [product] last year." Establishes credibility immediately. Numbers and specifics are more effective than vague claims.

### 5. Direct Hook
State the offer immediately: "50% off this week only." No buildup, no story. Works best for remarketing audiences who already know the brand.

### Hook Selection by Audience
- Cold audiences: question hook or problem hook (establish relevance)
- Warm audiences: social proof hook or surprise hook (build credibility)
- Hot audiences (remarketing): direct hook (make the offer)

## Thumbnail Optimization (In-Feed Ads)

In-feed ads rely on the thumbnail to earn the click. Thumbnail quality directly determines view rate.

### Thumbnail Best Practices
- **High contrast:** Stand out against YouTube's white/dark backgrounds
- **Clear imagery:** Readable at small mobile sizes
- **Text overlay:** Brief, readable at thumbnail size (3-5 words max)
- **Human face or product close-up:** Creates connection and specificity
- **Emotional or curiosity trigger:** Expression, question, surprising visual
- **Consistent branding:** Recognizable as part of the brand's visual system

### Common Thumbnail Mistakes
- Too much text (illegible at small sizes)
- Low contrast (blends into the page)
- Generic stock imagery (no differentiation)
- Clickbait that doesn't match the video content (drives views but not conversions)

## Creative Variations for Testing

When testing video creative, isolate one variable per test:

### Same Message, Different Hook
Keep the body and CTA identical. Test which hook type drives better view-through and conversion rates.

### Same Hook, Different CTA
Keep the opening and body identical. Test different calls to action: "Shop Now" vs. "Learn More" vs. "Get Your Free Sample."

### Same Product, Different Benefit Emphasis
Same product, same format. One version leads with convenience, another with price, another with quality. Identifies which benefit resonates most.

### Different Video Lengths of Same Content
Create 6s, 15s, and 30s versions of the same core message. Test which length drives the best cost-per-result for each objective.

### Format Testing
Same content adapted for horizontal (16:9) vs. vertical (9:16). Determines whether Shorts-style content outperforms traditional format for the target audience.

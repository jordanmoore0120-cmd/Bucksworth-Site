# Hook Strategies - 10 Hook Types with Examples and Benchmarks

## Why Hooks Matter

The hook is the first 3 seconds of a video or the first visual scan of a static ad. It determines whether someone stops scrolling. Everything else (body, CTA, landing page) is irrelevant if the hook fails.

**The math:** If your hook rate is 25% and you improve it to 35%, you've increased your addressable audience by 40% without spending an extra dollar. Hook optimization is the highest-leverage creative improvement you can make.

---

## Hook Type 1: The Bold Claim

**Pattern:** Lead with a provocative, specific statement that demands attention.

**Examples:**
- "This Slack bot saved our team 47 hours last month"
- "I replaced 3 SaaS tools with one AI coworker"
- "Our marketing team runs on 2 people and an AI"

**Benchmarks:**
- Hook rate: 30-45%
- Best for: Cold audiences, direct response
- Risk: Must deliver on the claim or you'll get high hook, low hold
- Format: Works across all formats (video, static, carousel first card)

**Rules:**
- Be specific (numbers > vague claims)
- Must be provably true (don't fabricate metrics)
- The claim should create a "how?" reaction
- Follow up immediately with proof

---

## Hook Type 2: The Pattern Interrupt

**Pattern:** Something visually or tonally unexpected that breaks the scroll pattern.

**Examples:**
- Opening frame is completely black, then text appears
- Ad starts with the sound of a Slack notification (triggers recognition)
- Founder appears to be in an unusual location
- The first frame looks like a meme, not an ad
- Text overlay in massive font: "STOP."

**Benchmarks:**
- Hook rate: 35-55%
- Best for: Cold audiences in competitive feeds
- Risk: High hook rate but can lead to low hold if the content doesn't match the energy
- Format: Video and static (especially Reels/Stories format)

**Rules:**
- The interrupt must relate to your product (random shock = clickbait)
- Transition smoothly from the interrupt to your message
- Test multiple interrupt types -- what works changes as audiences learn your patterns

---

## Hook Type 3: The Problem Call-Out

**Pattern:** Name the pain point directly. Make the viewer feel seen.

**Examples:**
- "If your Slack has 200+ unread messages right now, this is for you"
- "Tired of copying data between 5 different tools?"
- "You're paying $500/month for tools your team barely uses"
- "That feeling when you realize you forgot to follow up on 12 Slack threads"

**Benchmarks:**
- Hook rate: 28-40%
- Best for: All audiences, especially problem-aware prospects
- Risk: Can feel negative. Balance with quick pivot to solution.
- Format: Video (spoken or text overlay), Static (headline)

**Rules:**
- Be specific to YOUR audience's pain (not generic)
- The problem should feel urgent or frustrating
- Pivot to solution within 5 seconds (don't dwell on the problem)
- Use second person ("you") to make it personal

---

## Hook Type 4: The Social Proof Lead

**Pattern:** Start with evidence that other people (like the viewer) already use and love the product.

**Examples:**
- "10,000 teams switched to Viktor last month"
- "Rated #1 AI tool on Product Hunt"
- "Here's why every startup in YC is using this"
- Video opens with a montage of 5-second testimonial clips

**Benchmarks:**
- Hook rate: 25-35%
- Best for: Consideration and conversion stages
- Risk: Can feel generic if the social proof isn't specific or impressive
- Format: All formats, especially static and UGC

**Rules:**
- Numbers must be real and impressive to your audience
- Specificity beats scale ("12 YC companies" > "thousands of users" for a startup audience)
- Show the proof, don't just claim it (screenshots, logos, video clips)
- Pair with a credible source (Product Hunt badge, G2 rating, press logos)

---

## Hook Type 5: The Question

**Pattern:** Ask a question that the viewer instinctively wants to answer.

**Examples:**
- "What if your Slack could actually do your work?"
- "How much time does your team waste on repetitive tasks?"
- "Want to see the tool that replaced our project manager?"
- "What would you do with 10 extra hours per week?"

**Benchmarks:**
- Hook rate: 25-35%
- Best for: Awareness and consideration
- Risk: Weak questions get ignored. The question must trigger genuine curiosity.
- Format: Video (spoken or text), Static (headline), Carousel (first card)

**Rules:**
- The question should have an obvious desired answer ("yes" or "a lot")
- Avoid questions with a "no" answer ("Are you tired of X?" -> some people say "no" and scroll)
- Rhetorical questions work when they create "tell me more" energy
- Answer the question in the next 3-5 seconds

---

## Hook Type 6: The Demonstration

**Pattern:** Show the product doing something impressive in the first 3 seconds. No setup, no introduction.

**Examples:**
- Video opens mid-action: Viktor auto-responding to a Slack message
- Screen recording: typing a command, getting an instant result
- Side-by-side: manual process (slow) vs Viktor (instant)
- Before/after split screen (messy Slack vs organized Slack)

**Benchmarks:**
- Hook rate: 30-40%
- Best for: Product-aware audiences, consideration stage
- Risk: If the demo isn't visually impressive, it won't stop the scroll
- Format: Video (screen recording, product demo), Carousel (before/after)

**Rules:**
- Start with the "wow moment," not the setup
- The result should be visible in 3 seconds (no loading screens, no waiting)
- Annotate the screen (arrows, highlights) so it's comprehensible at a glance
- Works better when the task being automated is something the viewer does daily

## Hook Type 7: The Contrarian / Myth-Buster

**Pattern:** Challenge a belief the audience holds. Create cognitive dissonance.

**Examples:**
- "You don't need a bigger team. You need a smarter Slack."
- "Hiring is the worst way to scale a startup"
- "Project management tools are the problem, not the solution"
- "Stop training your team on new software. Let AI adapt to them."

**Benchmarks:**
- Hook rate: 30-45%
- Best for: Thought leaders, founders, opinionated brands
- Risk: Can alienate if the contrarian take is too aggressive or wrong
- Format: Video (founder/talking head), Static (bold text)

**Rules:**
- The contrarian take must be defensible (don't just be provocative for clicks)
- Follow immediately with evidence or reasoning
- Works best when the speaker has credibility (founder, expert, customer)
- The "myth" should be something the audience is actively frustrated by

---

## Hook Type 8: The "I Discovered" / Personal Story

**Pattern:** Frame the ad as a personal discovery story. Creates parasocial trust.

**Examples:**
- "I found this AI tool 3 weeks ago and it changed everything"
- "My team lead showed me this and I can't go back"
- "I was skeptical about AI tools until I tried Viktor"
- "Story time: how we cut our Slack time by 60%"

**Benchmarks:**
- Hook rate: 30-45% (especially in UGC format)
- Best for: Cold audiences, UGC content
- Risk: Feels inauthentic if the delivery is too polished or scripted
- Format: UGC video, Reels/Stories

**Rules:**
- Must feel genuinely spontaneous (even if scripted)
- The "discovery" should feel recent and exciting
- Include a specific detail that makes it credible ("3 weeks ago" not "recently")
- The person should be relatable to the target audience

---

## Hook Type 9: The Listicle / Number

**Pattern:** Promise a specific, countable set of insights. Creates a completeness urge.

**Examples:**
- "3 things I automated in Slack this week"
- "5 ways Viktor saves our team 10 hours/week"
- "The 1 tool every remote team needs in 2026"
- Carousel: "7 Slack hacks you're not using" (one per card)

**Benchmarks:**
- Hook rate: 25-35%
- Best for: Educational content, carousel format
- Risk: Overused format. The list items must be genuinely valuable or novel.
- Format: Carousel (natural fit), Video (countdown), Static (infographic)

**Rules:**
- Odd numbers outperform even numbers (3, 5, 7 > 4, 6, 8)
- Lower numbers for short-form (3-5), higher for carousel (5-10)
- Each item must be independently valuable (no filler)
- The first item should be the most surprising or valuable

---

## Hook Type 10: The Urgency / FOMO

**Pattern:** Create a sense that the viewer will miss out if they don't act.

**Examples:**
- "Viktor just launched a free tier -- but only for this month"
- "500 teams signed up this week. Here's what they know that you don't."
- "This pricing won't last past March"
- "Your competitors are already using this"

**Benchmarks:**
- Hook rate: 25-35%
- Best for: Conversion campaigns, promotions, limited offers
- Risk: False urgency destroys trust. Only use when the urgency is real.
- Format: Static (promotional), Video (announcement)

**Rules:**
- The urgency must be genuine (real deadline, real limit)
- Combine with a clear benefit (why should they care that it's limited?)
- "Your competitors" framing works well for B2B
- Don't overuse -- urgency loses power if every ad uses it

## Hook Selection Matrix

| Audience Temperature | Best Hook Types | Avoid |
|---------------------|----------------|-------|
| Ice cold (never heard of you) | Pattern Interrupt, Problem Call-Out, "I Discovered" | Social Proof (no context), Demo (no awareness) |
| Warm (aware of category) | Bold Claim, Question, Contrarian, Demo | Generic urgency |
| Hot (aware of product) | Social Proof, Demo, Urgency, Listicle | Problem Call-Out (they already know) |
| Retargeting | Urgency, Social Proof, Bold Claim | "I Discovered" (they already know you) |

## Hook Testing Protocol

1. **Identify your best-performing ad body** (the content after the hook)
2. **Create 5 hook variations** using different hook types from this list
3. **Test using 3:2:2 method:** 5 hooks (video) x 2 texts x 1 headline = 10 variants
4. **Budget:** 3x CPA/day for 48-72 hours
5. **Winner criteria:** Highest hook rate that also delivers acceptable CPA
6. **Iterate:** Take the winning hook type and create 3 variations within that type
7. **Scale:** Graduate the best to your Winners campaign via Post ID
